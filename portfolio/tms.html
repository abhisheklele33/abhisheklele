Orthon — Closed-Loop Sensing and Actuation System for Real-Time Plantar Pressure Offloading

Overview

Orthon is a smart insole system that autonomously detects high-pressure plantar regions and actively offloads them in real time to reduce diabetic foot ulcer risk. With over 537 million people living with diabetes globally — projected to reach 643 million by 2030 — and 19–34% developing foot ulcers during their lifetime, pressure offloading is a critical and underserved clinical need.

The system comprises a layered insole with a pressure sensor array, two actuator module designs (Nitinol lock-ring and Magnetorheological Fluid), a custom PCB, and a closed-loop PI control algorithm that continuously adjusts module height to redistribute plantar load.

Clinical targets met: offload pressure by 30–50% · withstand 800–1200 kPa peak plantar pressure · fit within 20 mm height constraint · identify heel strike, midstance, and toe liftoff.

Hardware Design

Sensing layer: A pressure sensor array maps plantar pressure distribution in real time. Data is processed via a low-pass moving average filter and mapped using Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) interpolation with O(h²) accuracy.

Nitinol Lock-Ring Module: A flexible nitinol-embedded TPU ring expands when current is applied, releasing the platform from a locked state into a spring-supported offloading position (15 mm → 12 mm drop). This provides passive, energy-efficient offloading.

MRF (Magnetorheological Fluid) Module: An electromagnet coil controls the viscosity of MRF through a piston orifice, enabling selective switching between rigid support and soft offloading states. Modules in soft state transfer load to adjacent rigid modules, achieving targeted redistribution.

Custom PCB: A bespoke PCB interfaces the sensor array and module controllers, handling power distribution, signal conditioning, and microcontroller communication.

Control Architecture

A descending-priority greedy selector algorithm identifies high-pressure spots from the mapped data and assigns offloading priority. A closed-loop PI controller then drives individual module height adjustments to reach target pressure thresholds. Real-time feedback is provided to both the patient (via a display interface) and the clinical team.

Bench testing confirmed that each excess pressure spike triggers a corresponding rise in module offloading distance, with the actuator dropping to redistribute the localized load — directly validating the offloading strategy.
