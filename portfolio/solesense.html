Design of a Two-Stage Helical-Spur Speed Reduction Gearbox
Type: Mechanical Design Project
Institution: MIT World Peace University, Pune
Domain: Machine Design / Power Transmission

Overview

This project involved the complete mechanical design and analysis of a two-stage speed reduction gearbox combining a helical gear first stage and a spur gear second stage. The gearbox was designed to achieve a specified speed reduction ratio while meeting strength, fatigue life, and geometric constraints.

The design process followed a systematic approach: gear geometry calculation → shaft design under combined bending and torsion → key and coupling design → bearing selection for rated life → housing design → tolerance stacking and fit analysis. Each component was modelled in SolidWorks and validated through analytical calculations and FEA.

Design Process

Gear design: Helical and spur gear geometry was calculated from the input speed, output speed, and power requirements. Module, number of teeth, helix angle (for stage 1), face width, and centre distance were determined to satisfy bending strength (Lewis equation) and surface durability (Hertz contact stress) criteria with appropriate safety factors.

Shaft design: Input, intermediate, and output shafts were designed for the combined bending moment and torque loading using the ASME shaft design equation. Shaft diameters were sized for both static and fatigue loading, with stress concentration factors at keyways and shoulders applied.

Bearing selection: Rolling element bearings were selected from standard catalogues based on the calculated radial and axial loads at each support, targeting a rated L10 life of 10,000 hours or greater.

FEA validation: Critical components — gear teeth under maximum tangential load and shaft sections at stress concentrations — were modelled and simulated in ANSYS to verify that maximum von Mises stress remained below allowable limits with appropriate safety margins.
