Overview

VisionX is a standalone wireless dual-module wearable system designed to provide intuitive spatial awareness to visually impaired individuals through multimodal haptic feedback. Rather than relying on auditory or visual cues, the system translates environmental distance and directionality into thermal and vibrotactile sensations — enabling users to navigate without diverting cognitive attention.

The sensing module is integrated into a spectacle frame and uses a VL53L5CX Time-of-Flight sensor with a BLE-enabled SEEED XIAO microcontroller. The actuation module is worn on the forearm and contains three spatially arranged Peltier thermoelectric elements (left, center, right) for directional thermal feedback, plus a Voice Coil Actuator (VCA) for critical proximity alerts.

System performance: 20–120 cm sensing range · BLE latency <50 ms · Thermal operating range 36–42°C · Pilot study feedback clarity rating 4.4/5.

System Architecture

Sensing module (spectacles): The VL53L5CX ToF sensor captures distance to nearby objects continuously. The SEEED XIAO nRF52840 microcontroller acquires and transmits this data wirelessly over BLE at low latency.

Actuation module (forearm): An Arduino Nano 33 BLE receives the distance data and executes the feedback mapping logic. Three heating elements positioned at left, center, and right arm positions are driven via a 4-channel MOSFET driver. A DHT11 sensor monitors actuator surface temperature, enforcing a closed-loop safety constraint (T ≤ 42°C). A Voice Coil Actuator fires when objects enter the critical proximity zone (<20 cm).

Feedback mapping: Objects beyond 100 cm produce no feedback. Between 50–100 cm, low-intensity thermal feedback (36–39°C) is applied to the directionally corresponding element. Between 20–50 cm, medium intensity (39–42°C). Below 20 cm, a VCA vibrotactile alert fires. PWM modulation creates smooth, gradient thermal transitions.

Pilot Study Results

A 5-participant blindfolded pilot study evaluated usability, feedback clarity, and ergonomics. Participants identified obstacle direction and proximity level using haptic feedback alone. Results showed strong performance across all metrics: usability 4.2/5, feedback clarity 4.4/5, ergonomics 3.8/5. Average response time was approximately 1.5 seconds.

The combination of thermal (directional) and vibrotactile (urgency) cues allowed users to reliably parse two dimensions of spatial information simultaneously without confusion between modalities — a key advantage over single-modality systems.
